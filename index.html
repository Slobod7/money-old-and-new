[Vanguard found](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/cost_averaging_invest_now_or_temporarily_hold_your_cash.pdf) that investing a windfall at once beat spreading it over three months about two-thirds of the time, including in European stocks. Spreading wins when a crash follows soon after. The calculator’s “Every month” option answers a different question: what saving the same amount from each month’s income would have become. - column: numbers question: "Why did German bonds do so badly?" answer: >- Because interest rates rose from below zero. When new bonds pay more, older ones paying less fall in price. In August 2019 a 10-year Bund yielded −0.65%; by October 2023, 2.8%. Over that time the calculator’s Bund lost 27% in euros, or 38% after inflation. Germany never missed a payment: the risk was rates and inflation, not default. - column: numbers question: "Where do the figures come from, and what’s left out?" answer: >- From public sources: the ECB (deposit rates, German bond yields, exchange rates, and Eurostat’s euro area inflation index), the World Bank (gold), Kenneth French’s Data Library (US and European stocks, dividends reinvested) and Coin Metrics (Bitcoin, from July 2010). These are index figures, so fees, taxes and the cost of storing gold are left out. Your own account or fund would differ, mostly by its costs. - column: money question: "Do savings accounts beat inflation?" answer: >- Not at the moment. In August 2026, euro area banks paid households an average 2.13% on deposits fixed for up to a year and 0.29% on money they can withdraw any time, while prices rose 3.8% in the year to September. Since January 2000, the average fixed deposit has returned [deposit_2000] a year after inflation, before tax. - column: money question: "How do ECB rate decisions affect my savings?" answer: >- Partly, and with a delay. The ECB sets the rate banks earn on money they leave with it: 2.50% since 16 September 2026, its second rise this year. Banks pass some of that on to new fixed-term deposits but barely touch everyday accounts. Between May and August 2026, the average short fixed-deposit rate rose from 1.92% to 2.13%; the everyday-account rate, from 0.27% to 0.29%. - column: money question: "Is gold or Bitcoin a hedge against inflation?" answer: >- Not reliably: both have had long stretches of losing value after inflation. Gold has returned [gold_1999] a year after inflation since January 1999, but lost 35% of its real value between September 2012 and December 2013. Bitcoin lost 73% after inflation from October 2021 to December 2022, as euro area inflation hit a record 10.6%. Euro area inflation, 3.8% in September 2026, is a weighted average of about 700 goods and services across 21 countries. Your rate depends on what you buy: rent, fuel and food weigh more for some households, and the cost of owning a home isn’t counted yet. National rates differ too: in September, 3.3% in Germany and 6.5% in Romania, which isn’t in the euro. - column: money question: "What’s in the Sunday letter, and will you tell me what to buy?" answer: >- It’s a free English-language letter, written from Bucharest, on how ECB decisions, inflation and currencies reach your savings, and where old money (cash, deposits, bonds, gold, stocks) and new money (Bitcoin, stablecoins) fit.
