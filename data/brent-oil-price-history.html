How to read this chart

The series is EIA’s Europe Brent Spot Price FOB (free on board), a crude-oil benchmark, rather than a futures contract. EIA describes its observations as daily closing spot prices.

  • Compare the same periods. 1Y uses daily observations; longer views use monthly averages. The “Since 1990” summary compares annual averages. An endpoint change is not the average price over the whole range.
  • Read the frequency of a high or low. A monthly extreme is an extreme among averages, not an intraday price. Event notes quote daily prices, so they can differ from the monthly point carrying the marker.
  • Check incomplete periods. “So far” or an average “to” a date marks a partial month or year.
  • Today’s dollars are still dollars. Prices are scaled by US CPI to {{ brent.cpi_to | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }}. This is inflation adjustment, not euro conversion.

Hover, tap or drag to read a point. With the chart focused, use the arrow keys, Page Up/Down, Home or End. “Show the data” contains annual averages; without JavaScript, the line also uses annual figures. Historical closes and averages are not executable quotes.

Why the euro move can differ

USD per barrel÷ dollars per euro= EUR per barrel

The chart stays in dollars. The calculator offers euros.

Oil and the exchange rate both matter to the euro cost of a barrel.

A weaker euro
Raises the euro cost if the dollar oil price is unchanged.
A stronger euro
Lowers the euro cost if the dollar oil price is unchanged.
How the dates and rates are matched

Each purchase uses the first available EIA daily price of its month and that date’s ECB reference rate, or the latest preceding rate within seven calendar days. The final valuation pairs the latest daily oil observation with the rate on or before that date. Euro calculations begin in January 1999.

These are purchase-date and valuation-date conversions, not monthly averages. ECB rates, quoted in dollars per euro, are normally published around 16:00 CET after a procedure around 14:10 CET. They are information rates, not transaction prices; pairing them with EIA closes does not create a simultaneous tradable oil/FX quote.

From crude oil to household prices

An oil-price move reaches different household costs at different speeds.

  • More than the oil price

    Pump prices also include refining, distribution and retail costs and margins, excise duties and VAT. There is no fixed percentage pass-through from crude oil.

    Compare EU fuel prices →

  • Bills move at their own pace

    Gas prices, the generation mix, network charges, taxes, contracts and national regulation all matter. Retail prices can respond with a delay.

    Read the ECB’s explanation →

  • One part of a wider basket

    HICP measures a weighted basket of goods and services. Oil affects fuel and production and transport costs, but this chart cannot measure its total inflation effect.

A few historical moves in context

Three episodes to help orient the chart. Each has several drivers; a marker does not explain an entire move.

  1. 2014–16

    EIA links the late-2014 fall to production exceeding demand, and describes high production and inventories weighing on prices in 2016.

  2. 2020

    Pandemic responses sharply reduced petroleum demand. The negative-price episode was in WTI futures; it should not be read as a negative Brent spot observation.

  3. 2022

    EIA identifies Russia’s invasion of Ukraine and low global inventories as contributors to the first-half rise, followed by demand concerns and increased supply in the second half.

Sources & method

Observations through {{ brent.brent_to | date: "%-d %B %Y" }}{% if brent.eia_release %} · EIA release {{ brent.eia_release | date: "%-d %B %Y" }}{% endif %}. Explanatory notes checked {{ page.content_checked | date: "%-d %B %Y" }}.

Oil prices
U.S. Energy Information Administration, Europe Brent Spot Price FOB (RBRTE), in US dollars per barrel. Daily closing spot prices and published monthly and annual averages. EIA names Refinitiv, an LSEG business, as its underlying source.
Inflation
US Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI via FRED, seasonally adjusted (CPIAUCSL), to {{ brent.cpi_to | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }}. Euro calculations use Eurostat’s euro area HICP, changing composition, via the ECB (HICP.M.U2.N.000000.4D0.INX), to {{ brent.hicp_to | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }}.
Exchange rates
European Central Bank reference rates, US dollars per euro (EXR.D.USD.EUR.SP00.A). Currency conversion and inflation adjustment are this site’s calculations, not provider quotes.
Averages and missing observations

EIA’s published monthly and annual averages are unweighted means of daily closing spot prices. Until publication, the latest month or year uses the mean of available daily observations and is labelled as partial. Missing oil observations are omitted, not filled with zero; the line connects available points.

Inflation adjustment multiplies a price by the latest index divided by its month’s index; the annual table uses the year’s mean CPI. Update schedule and reuse

The site’s scheduled refresh is monthly, on the 18th at 06:30 UTC, with manual updates possible. The update date changes when the dataset changes. Failed downloads or validation leave the previous dataset in place; provider revisions can change history at a later refresh. This is not a live feed.

EIA permits reuse of its data with dated acknowledgement; third-party protected material has separate terms. See also the BLS reuse policy and ECB attribution and reuse terms.
