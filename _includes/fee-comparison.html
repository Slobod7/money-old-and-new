How the fees are charged

Monthly balance = previous balance × [(1 + return) × (1 − fee)]1/12 + monthly payment.

  • This spreads each year’s fee across its months. It’s an approximation: real funds charge in their own ways, and a published ongoing charge or TER isn’t automatically the whole cost of investing.
  • The starting amount is invested at once. Each monthly payment arrives after that month’s growth and fee, so it bears a fee only from the month it goes in, never for earlier years.
  • Both scenarios use the same gross return. In practice, funds with different fees can also earn different returns before fees.
  • Today’s money means prices at the start of the scenario. Balances are divided by the inflation factor at the end, and each payment by the factor at the month it goes in, so both are on the same price basis.
  • Tax, dealing costs, fixed platform fees and market ups and downs aren’t included. It’s an illustration, not advice.
