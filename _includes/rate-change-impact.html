What a change in your bank’s rate means

Enter the rates your own bank quotes, not the ECB’s. Banks set their own customer rates, and an ECB move needn’t reach yours by the same amount or at the same time: see the ECB’s explanation of the transmission mechanism. These are scenarios from your figures, not a forecast of ECB decisions or a product recommendation. The opening figures are examples, and no live bank data is used.

Example: €10,000 at an effective 2% a year earns €200 in a year, and at 2.5% it earns €250. A €200,000 loan over 25 years costs €948 a month at a nominal 3% and €1,001 at 3.5%. Enable JavaScript to try your own figures.

Assumptions and calculation

Savings. Rates are annual effective rates: what the balance earns over a full year, with any compounding within the year already counted, so nothing is converted. Interest = balance × rate, for one year on an unchanged balance. Tax is your own assumption, applied once to the interest; the rules depend on your country and circumstances.

Loan. Rates are nominal annual rates divided by 12. Each payment is worked out for the same principal and remaining term, as if that rate were fixed until the loan is repaid: payment = P × m ÷ [1 − (1 + m)−n], where P is the principal, m the monthly rate and n the number of months. At zero interest, payment = P ÷ n.

The two kinds of rate aren’t the same, so don’t compare the savings and loan figures directly: a nominal 4% with monthly payments is an effective 4.07% a year.
