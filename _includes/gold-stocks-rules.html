A historical comparison, not a buying signal. Changing the window can change which asset finished ahead. These are source-based illustrations, not the returns of a tradeable fund or a quote you could have dealt at.

Conventions and sources
  • Every contribution enters at the end of the selected month, including both start and end months for monthly buying. The final contribution earns no return. Both assets use exactly the same months; a missing observation prevents the comparison.
  • Gold uses the World Bank Pink Sheet’s monthly average US-dollar price per troy ounce divided by the ECB’s average USD-per-euro rate for that month. The same convention values it at the end. It is an average-price proxy, not a month-end execution price. Gold pays no dividend.
  • US stocks are the French library’s value-weighted US broad-market series from CRSP. Developed Europe is its regional value-weighted market portfolio. These are research benchmarks. The engine adds the one-month risk-free return back to Mkt−RF and compounds monthly returns in US dollars, then converts levels to euros at the ECB’s last rate of each month. Neither is currency-hedged.
  • Stock returns include the source’s dividends and capital gains. The US file uses CRSP’s CIZ convention, compounding daily returns with dividends reinvested on ex-dates; legacy FIZ files used month-end reinvestment. The developed-Europe source specifies dividends included. No separate dividend is added here.
  • Real mode expresses every balance and contribution in the selected end month’s euros using euro area HICP. Nominal mode leaves them unadjusted. Largest falls and recovery use the same mode and monthly observations; intramonth falls are not measured. Annual returns need at least a year of history.
  • Brokerage, bid–offer spreads, gold dealer premiums, storage, insurance, fund fees, taxes and trading or currency-conversion costs are excluded.
