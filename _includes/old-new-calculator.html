    {%- capture cc_rules %}
    • Each purchase is made at the end of a month, at that month’s closing value, and today’s value is at the end of {{ cc_to }}. Dollar prices convert to euros at the ECB’s last rate of the month. Gold is the exception: it uses the month’s average price and average rate, because that’s the only free monthly gold price.
    • Bank deposit: the average rate euro area banks paid households on new deposits fixed for up to a year, as if the money were put in again every month. Figures start in {{ df.from.deposit | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }}.{% for m in df.deposit_filled %} The rate for {{ m | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }} isn’t out yet, so the month before’s is used.{% endfor %}
    • German bonds: worked out from the 10-year Bund’s monthly average yield. Each month the bond is valued at the new yield, then swapped for a new 10-year bond.
    • Stocks: the whole market, with dividends reinvested. US stocks are every company listed in the US, which move almost exactly with the S&P 500. European stocks are the developed markets of Europe, including the UK and Switzerland.
    • Bitcoin: prices start in {{ df.from.bitcoin | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }}.
    • In today’s money, every figure is adjusted with euro area consumer prices (HICP, to {{ df.hicp_to | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }}).
    • The biggest fall is the largest drop from a high to a later low in the asset itself, after you start. It’s checked at the end of each month, so falls within a month were deeper.
    • Fees, taxes and the cost of keeping gold safe aren’t included.
