Minimum wages
Eurostat, monthly minimum wages (earn_mw_cur): each country’s national minimum wage on 1 January and 1 July, gross, as a monthly amount, in euros, in purchasing power standards and in national currency. {{ mw.with_rate }} EU countries have one; Eurostat flags the other {{ mw.no_national.size }} as having none.
Monthly amounts
Eurostat’s, from each country’s own rate. A rate set by the hour or the week is multiplied by the hours that country supplies: 38.8 a week for Germany, 39 for Ireland, 35 for France and 36 for the Netherlands; Malta’s weekly rate × 52 ÷ 12. A rate paid 14 times a year, in Greece, Spain and Portugal, is multiplied by 14 and divided by 12. Eurostat’s notes, read {{ mw.method_checked | date: "%-d %B %Y" }}. This page applies no conversion of its own and works out no hourly rate.
Euros
Outside the euro, Eurostat converts the national amount at the exchange rate at the end of the month before, so a currency move changes the euro figure when the wage hasn’t changed.
Purchasing power
Eurostat’s own figures in purchasing power standards, converted with purchasing power parities for household spending from the latest year it had. Eurostat marks them as estimates. This page doesn’t work them out itself: dividing by Eurostat’s separate price levels gives different answers.
Ranks
This site’s ordering of Eurostat’s figures, among the {{ mw.with_rate }} countries with a national minimum wage. Equal figures share a rank and the next rank skips the shared places. Places moved is the rank in euros minus the rank by purchasing power.
Comparison
This site’s arithmetic: one country’s published figure against another’s, as a percentage of the second, one measure at a time. Euros are never set against purchasing power standards.
Map
Outlines made with Natural Earth, simplified, on an equal-area projection. Overseas territories and the smallest islands are left out of the drawing. Each view has five shades in equal bands, set afresh at every update from that date’s lowest and highest figures. Hatching marks a country with no national minimum wage.
Ireland and Spain
The legal rates from the Irish Statute Book and the Workplace Relations Commission, and from Spain’s official gazette, the BOE. Typed by hand and checked {{ mn.checked | date: "%-d %B %Y" }}; the data update doesn’t change them.

Update schedule and reuse

The saved data is refreshed by a scheduled download on the 18th of each month, with manual updates possible. Eurostat publishes new figures twice a year, in late January and late July, so a new date shows from the first download after that. This is not a live feed.

Eurostat allows reuse of its data with the source named. The ranks, the places moved, the comparisons and the map’s shades are this site’s work on those figures; Eurostat is not responsible for them. Natural Earth’s map data is in the public domain.
