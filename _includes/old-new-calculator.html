{%- if include.interpretation %}

The short answer

One amount. Three ways to read it.

Equivalent purchasing power
The euros needed at the endpoint to match the starting basket.
Cumulative price change
How much the basket’s price changed between those months.
Loss of buying power
How much less the unchanged cash amount can buy.

Why are the two percentages different?

They use different denominators. With starting index H₀ and ending index H₁:

  • Equivalent amount = amount × H₁/H₀
  • Cumulative price change = H₁/H₀ − 1
  • Buying-power loss = 1 − H₀/H₁

A negative buying-power loss means a gain. These are changes over the whole period, not annual rates.

    • Each purchase is made at the end of a month, at that month’s closing value, {% if cc_dates %}and the balance is valued at the end of your selected end month.{% else %}and today’s value is at the end of {{ cc_to }}.{% endif %} Dollar prices convert to euros at the ECB’s last rate of the month. Gold is the exception: it uses the month’s average price and average rate, as supplied by the World Bank Pink Sheet.
    • Bank deposit: the average rate euro area banks paid households on new deposits fixed for up to a year, as if the money were put in again every month. Figures start in {{ df.from.deposit | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }}, and the ECB marks those for 2000 to 2002 as estimates.{% for m in df.deposit_filled %} The rate for {{ m | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }} isn’t out yet, so the month before’s is used.{% endfor %}
    • German bonds: worked out from the 10-year Bund’s monthly average yield. Each month the bond is valued at the new yield, then swapped for a new 10-year bond.
    • Stocks: the whole market, with dividends reinvested. US stocks use the French library’s value-weighted US broad-market series from CRSP. European stocks use its developed-Europe market portfolio.
    • Bitcoin: prices start in {{ df.from.bitcoin | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }}.
    • {% if cc_dates %}Real figures use euro area HICP and are expressed in the selected end month’s euros.{% else %}In today’s money, every figure is adjusted with euro area consumer prices (HICP, to {{ df.hicp_to | append: "-01" | date: "%B %Y" }}).{% endif %}
    • The biggest fall is the largest drop from a high to a later low in the asset itself, after you start. It’s checked at the end of each month, so falls within a month were deeper.
    • Fees, taxes and the cost of keeping gold safe aren’t included.
